Privacy policy

Your saved stops stay on your devices.

Your location is used on the device, and never sent anywhere.

Last updated 18 August 2026 · Applies to StopSpotter 1.0 on iPhone and Apple Watch

  • No accounts
  • No analytics
  • No tracking
  • No ads
  • No third-party SDKs
  • No profile of you
The whole policy in one sentence

StopSpotter has no user accounts and never asks who you are; saved stops, nicknames and preferences are stored only on your device; your location is used on the device and is not sent anywhere; and the one server the app talks to is asked which stop, never who is asking.

What StopSpotter stores, and where

Saved stops, names and preferences are written to storage on your device and stay there. There is no cloud sync and no account, so there is no copy anywhere else. Deleting the app deletes them.

Location

If you allow it, StopSpotter uses your location to list stops near you, and to point the nearest-stop widget at the right stop. It asks for when-in-use access only: there is no background location, no significant-change monitoring, and nothing that follows you around. That lookup runs on your device, against a transit database the app carries. Your coordinates are not sent to Reforged Studio or to anyone else — the question "which stops are near this point" is answered without the network.

Your most recent coordinate is kept on the device, inside StopSpotter's own storage, so the widget can still name the stop you were at when it can't get a fresh fix. It is discarded after 24 hours, and immediately if you turn location off or switch the app to approximate location. Nothing keeps a history of where you have been.

You can change or revoke this at any time in Settings › Apps › StopSpotter. Without it, everything except nearby stops and the nearest-stop widget still works — you can search for stops by name.

Network requests

StopSpotter talks to exactly one address on the internet: stopspotter-api.reforged.studio, a small server run by Reforged Studio. Your device does not contact NJ Transit or the Port Authority itself; that server does, on behalf of everyone at once, and passes back the departures. NJ Transit's licence requires that shape — an app may not send its users' devices to their systems directly.

What the request contains: a stop identifier. Not your location, not your name, not an account, and no identifier for you or your device. There is nothing in it that could say which requests came from the same person.

Like any request to any website, it arrives carrying the IP address your connection is using. The server keeps no record tying a request to a person or a device, and builds no profile of anyone; its hosting provider, Cloudflare, processes the request in the ordinary course of delivering it and may hold short-lived operational logs of its own. The transit agencies, at the far end, see the server — not you.

The server does keep one running record, and it is worth naming precisely: a log of observed departures — the times boats and trains were predicted to arrive, taken from the system-wide snapshot it already fetches, so that timetable gaps can be reconstructed later. It is a record of vehicles, not of people. No request, device or person appears in it, and it is written on a fixed schedule whether anyone is using the app or not.

Schedule data

Timetables, route names and stop locations are carried inside the app, and reading them needs no network at all — which is why there is still an answer on screen when everything else is down.

So that a timetable change doesn't have to wait for an App Store update, the app also checks the same server for a newer copy of that database: at most once a day, on Wi-Fi, in the background. It's a file download and it says nothing about you.

Purchases

StopSpotter+ is purchased through the App Store. Purchases are handled entirely by Apple, under Apple's own privacy policy. Reforged Studio never sees your payment details, and there is no purchase server of ours involved — the app asks Apple whether the purchase exists and records the answer on your device, so the widget knows. What it stores is one bit of entitlement, not a receipt.

Apple Watch

Your saved stops reach the watch from your iPhone over Apple's own device-to-device sync, the same channel any paired app uses. Nothing goes via Reforged Studio and nothing goes via a third party. The watch asks the same server for departures the phone does, and that request has the same shape: a stop identifier and nothing about you.

Diagnostics

StopSpotter carries a small diagnostics recorder used while the app is being developed. In the version you download from the App Store it records nothing at all — the recording is compiled out, not merely switched off. Where it does run, everything it collects stays on the device and leaves it only if you deliberately export it and send it to us.

Children

StopSpotter asks nobody who they are, and that includes children. It is a general-purpose transit utility and is not directed at children.

Changes to this policy

If the app's behaviour changes in a way that affects privacy, this policy will be updated and the date above revised before that version ships.
