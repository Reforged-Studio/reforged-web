Common questions

Which agencies and modes are supported?

Five services, in one app: NJ Transit bus, NJ Transit rail, NJ Transit light rail, PATH, and NY Waterway ferries — all five with live departures where the operator is reporting them, and the published timetable underneath when they aren't.

Not New York City Transit, not Amtrak, and not private carriers.

What's the difference between “updated 2 min ago” and “Schedule data”?

It's the difference between what the operator is reporting right now and what the timetable says is supposed to happen.

updated 2 min ago means the times came from a live feed, and tells you when they arrived. Schedule data means the published timetable, built into the app — no network needed. You'll see it whenever there's no live prediction to show. It means: this is the timetable, not a prediction.

Individual rows say Live or Scheduled for the same reason, because one stop can be a mix of both. A Scheduled time is a real countdown — it just can't know that one particular bus is running late.

Where does the data come from?

Predictions from NJ Transit data. Derived from publicly available PATH data. Schedule data and predictions derived from publicly available NY Waterway data. Timetables come from the operators' published schedule feeds and are carried inside the app, so there is still an answer when the live feeds are down. Data availability is not guaranteed.

StopSpotter is an independent app. It is not endorsed by, affiliated with, or supported by NJ Transit, the Port Authority of NY & NJ, or NY Waterway.

How do I put a stop on the Home Screen?

Touch and hold an empty part of the Home Screen, tap Edit → Add Widget, find StopSpotter, and pick a size.

Then — and this is the step people miss — touch and hold the widget you just placed, tap Edit Widget, and choose the saved stop from the list. A widget you've just dropped hasn't been pointed at anything yet.

Place as many as you like and stack them in one spot. Bear in mind iOS budgets background refreshes per app rather than per widget, so the more you place, the less often each updates on its own.

Why does my widget show my nearest stop instead of the one I saved?

Because it hasn't been pointed at one yet. Every widget starts on Nearest stop, which is the free setting and needs no setting up at all.

Pointing a widget at a stop you saved — your routes, your direction, your name for it — is what StopSpotter+ buys. Until then the widget still works; it just answers “what's leaving near me” rather than “when's my bus”.

How does a widget refresh?

It updates itself, on iOS's schedule rather than ours. A widget is meant to be glanceable, so it keeps a recent answer on screen and refreshes in the background when the system allows it.

That's why every widget says how old its data is. If you want the answer for right now, tap the refresh arrow in its corner and it fetches immediately. Past fifteen minutes it says outright that it may not reflect real-time data.

My widget says “Schedule data” even though the app shows live times.

Give it a minute or two. A widget builds itself the moment you place it, and if that moment lands before the app has managed its first live fetch, the widget quite correctly shows timetable times and says so.

It doesn't stay that way. A widget that came up on schedule data asks iOS to rebuild it within a couple of minutes rather than waiting for its usual slot. You can also tap the refresh arrow. If it's still on schedule data after five minutes or so, the live feed genuinely has nothing for that stop right now.

I tapped Edit Widget and the list of stops just spins.

Restart your iPhone. That fixes it.

The list of stops a widget can show is built on your device, from a file the app has already written — there's no network involved and nothing to wait for. When it spins anyway, what's stuck is iOS's own record of the app's widget settings, and a restart clears it.

If a restart doesn't fix it, that's genuinely new — please write, and say what you'd done just before it started.

Why did adding a widget ask for my location?

Because the widget's out-of-the-box setting is Nearest stop, and iOS asks for that permission once, for the app as a whole, the first time any StopSpotter widget is added.

There's only one StopSpotter widget, and iOS has no way to ask per setting — so the prompt appears even if you're planning to point the widget at a stop you saved. You can say no. The widget still works pointed at a saved stop.

How do I get StopSpotter on my Apple Watch?

It comes with the iPhone app. If your watch installs apps automatically it will already be there; otherwise open the Watch app on your iPhone, scroll to StopSpotter, and turn on Show App on Apple Watch.

The watch app is part of the free app. It lists every combo you've saved, with live times and the same freshness line the phone shows.

How do I add a StopSpotter card to my Smart Stack?

On the watch face, turn the Digital Crown down to open the Smart Stack, press and hold, tap +, and choose StopSpotter. Then pick which of your saved combos the card should show.

That last part — pointing a card at a combo you saved — is the StopSpotter+ half, exactly as it is on the Home Screen.

My watch card says “Unlock in StopSpotter on iPhone”.

That card is pointed at one of your saved combos, which is a StopSpotter+ feature, and the purchase hasn't reached the watch yet.

StopSpotter+ is bought on the iPhone — there's no separate watch purchase and nothing extra to pay. Open StopSpotter on the phone (buying it, or tapping Restore Purchases in Settings if you already own it), and the watch picks it up from the phone shortly after. The free watch app and its nearest-stop card keep working either way.

What do I choose when I save a stop?

The stop itself, and then as much or as little as you want on top of it: which routes you care about, which direction, and a name of your own.

Narrowing to one or two routes keeps the list short and useful; leaving it open shows everything departing that stop. The name you give it is what appears on the widget, so “Morning bus” beats a stop code and a direction string.

Can I reorder my saved stops? What are the chips at the top?

Yes — on the iPhone, press and hold a saved stop and drag it. There's no Edit button to find first. The order you set is the order the widget's picker offers, so putting your morning stop first is worth doing. On the watch, reordering appears once you have more than one card to arrange.

The row of chips above the list — Bus, Rail, Light Rail, PATH, Ferry — appears when your saved stops span more than one mode, and filters the list to one of them. It's a view, not a setting: nothing is deleted and the widget is unaffected.

Which gate is my bus at Port Authority?

Where NJ Transit publishes a gate for a departure, StopSpotter puts it on the row next to the route, reading Gate 213 or, when the operator says so rather than naming a number, Check board.

Some departures show no gate at all, and that is not a fault. NJ Transit does not publish one for every trip — gates are not assigned everywhere, are not always given in advance, and can change after they're published. StopSpotter shows a gate when there is one to show and stays quiet when there isn't, rather than guessing. Check the terminal signage before you board.

A station is showing no departures at all.

Most often that means nothing is scheduled to leave from it soon. Trips that terminate at a station don't produce departures there — at the end of a line, or late at night, an empty board is the correct answer rather than a broken one.

If a direction filter is set on a saved stop, that narrows it too. Open the stop itself and switch to all departures to see everything.

What is free, and what does StopSpotter+ cost?

The app is free, and not a limited version — search, nearby stops, as many saved stops as you like, full detail on any of them, all five services, live and scheduled times, and the watch app. Free also includes a widget on the Home Screen and a card on the watch, both showing whichever stop you're nearest to, which need no setting up at all.

StopSpotter+ buys one thing: pointing those at a stop you saved, with your routes, your direction and your name for it — on iPhone and Apple Watch alike. It is $2.99, once. No subscription, nothing to renew, and no ads or account either way.

I bought StopSpotter+ on another device / I reinstalled the app.

Open Settings in the app and tap Restore Purchases, or reach the same button from the StopSpotter+ screen. It's a one-time purchase tied to your Apple Account, so it comes back on any device signed in to the same account, and the watch picks it up from the phone.

Refunds are Apple's to give, not ours — go to reportaproblem.apple.com, sign in, and choose the purchase. We never see your payment details and cannot process a refund on your behalf. If something about the app is the reason you want one, we'd like to hear about it either way.
